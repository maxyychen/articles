# The Power of Lifecycle Thinking

Two weeks into studying for my ITIL 4 Foundation exam, one diagram stopped me cold. Not because it was complex — but because it reframed something I thought I already understood.

The ITIL Continual Improvement model doesn't just describe how change happens. It insists that change is never finished. Every cycle ends with one question: How do we keep the momentum going? — and feeds that answer directly back into a renewed vision.

That's not unique to IT. In data management, skipping the archive and deletion phases leaves you buried in technical debt. In project management, skipping post-closure review means you repeat the same mistakes in the next sprint.

Every lifecycle only works if it's actually a cycle — not a line with a finish tape.

The most dangerous question isn't "did we succeed?" It's stopping there and never asking "how do we sustain this?"

What lifecycle in your organisation has no feedback loop? That's probably where the problems are quietly compounding.
