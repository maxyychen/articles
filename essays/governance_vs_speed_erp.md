# The Real Fight Isn't AI vs. Low-Code — It's Governance vs. Speed

For decades, ERP has run on the same loop: the business explains what it needs, IT builds it, and the business waits. Every change goes through a queue.

Low-code and no-code tools showed up to shorten that loop — drag-and-drop canvases, prebuilt connectors, visual logic instead of raw code. They made workflows easier to build. But they never changed who was in charge. A business owner could assemble a process inside the boxes IT gave them. IT still owned the platform, the templates, and the escalation path for anything the canvas couldn't handle.

Agentic AI breaks that pattern. It's not just an easier toolkit — it's a different way of building altogether. Instead of assembling pieces inside a fixed set of options, a business owner can now describe the outcome they want, and the system figures out how to build it. That's no longer simplification. That's a shift in who actually authors the system.

Which is why I think the industry is arguing about the wrong thing. Everyone's debating whether AI will kill low-code platforms or make them bigger. The real question is simpler: who gets to hold the pen?

Here's what's actually changing underneath. Low-code platforms are being rebuilt around a governed backend — data model, permissions, audit trail — that AI agents can act through safely. In practice, that means a business owner can describe their ideal process in plain language, something like "when a vendor invoice goes over $10K, route it to my regional manager before finance sees it," and get back a working, governed system. No ticket, no backlog, no six-week wait for an IT sprint to open up.

Most people already building on these platforms are non-technical business users, not IT. Agentic AI doesn't start that trend — it removes the last real barrier to it. That barrier was never "can I use a drag-and-drop tool." It was "can I just describe what I want and trust the system to build it right."

So does IT disappear? No. But the job changes completely.

Moving at the speed of conversation is only safe if the guardrails are already built into the platform: permission limits, approval thresholds, audit logs, checks that keep one person from both creating and approving a transaction. Someone still has to design those rails — that's IT's new job. Not building every workflow by hand, but defining the boundaries agentic AI has to operate inside: what a business owner's AI is allowed to touch, what always needs a human to sign off, what gets logged and reviewed. Support and governance, not gatekeeping.

That's the real fight in enterprise software right now — not AI replacing developers, but governance trying to keep pace with how fast business owners can now move on their own. The winners won't be the best coders. They'll be the business owners who can describe their own workflows clearly, backed by IT teams that can build the guardrails fast enough to keep up with them.
